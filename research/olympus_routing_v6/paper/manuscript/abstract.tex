\begin{abstract}
We evaluate a bounded successor to five unsuccessful Olympus routing primary
endpoints. The model blends a frozen, development-selected reference with a
learned alternative using an example-dependent intervention penalty and
worst-group training. Seven correction variants and strong fixed, uniform,
single-expert, direct, and constant-blend controls isolate competing explanations.
Across five generated datasets and two optimizer seeds per dataset, adaptive
robust routing achieves 99.619\% accuracy on a reserved synthetic shift,
versus 99.697\% for the development-selected control. The paired difference is
$-0.078$ percentage points (descriptive 95\% dataset-cluster interval
$[-0.410,0.117]$), failing the prospectively required two-point gain.
All familiar-regime nonregression criteria pass. The method improves familiar
corruption accuracy by 0.605 points over its original reference, but gains no
advantage over stronger continued-training controls. Under reserved shift it
rescues nine reference errors while introducing 22 new errors across ten paired
runs. The control's near-ceiling accuracy leaves only 0.303 points of possible
gain, limiting the benchmark's ability to demonstrate the registered practical
improvement. All forty protected reports, checkpoints, raw predictions, and
exact replay checks are retained. The outcome is negative for the registered
claim, not a general impossibility result or a full-architecture capability test.
\end{abstract}
