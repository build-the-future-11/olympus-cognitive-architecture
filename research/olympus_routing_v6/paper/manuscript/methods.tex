\section{Introduction}
Learned routing can degrade an already strong ensemble when the gate learns a
relation between confidence, representation, and correctness that does not
survive a change in observations. In the preserved Olympus sequence, five
routing primary endpoints failed their declared improvement criteria. Some
secondary interventions helped, including expanded corruption coverage and a
residual temporal predictor. These observations motivate a narrower question:
can a learned correction retain a strong reference while making useful,
example-dependent changes under a reserved synthetic perturbation?

Our testable statement is: existing Olympus gates struggle to outperform strong
controls under changed corruption configurations; a penalized, example-dependent
blend around a frozen reference may improve the rescue-to-harm balance because
it can reduce intervention where the alternative is unreliable. This is a
hypothesis, not a guarantee. We compare anchoring, input dependence, and
worst-group training in a fixed study with fresh source identities. The
contribution is an empirical decomposition and reproducible negative-result
continuity, not a new universal mixture architecture.

\section{Related work and novelty boundary}
Input-dependent expert mixtures are established by Jacobs et al.\ \cite{jacobs1991}.
Learning a combiner from held-out predictions is the premise of stacked
generalization\ \cite{wolpert1992}. Our two frozen banks and disjoint meta-fitting
partitions implement that principle conservatively; they are not nested
cross-fitting. The proposed convex blend is also ordinary probability mixing.

Sagawa et al.\ \cite{sagawa2020} show that small worst-group training loss does
not ensure worst-group generalization and emphasize regularization. Our maximum
of three minibatch losses is distinct from their adaptive-weight group-DRO
algorithm. Ovadia et al.\ \cite{ovadia2019} motivate checking calibration under
shift rather than trusting confidence alone. Neither paper proves improvement
for this generator or this gate.

Learning to defer\ \cite{mozannar2020} studies a classifier's decision to use
an expert with a consistent surrogate. We instead train a soft probability
mixture and do not inherit its consistency theorem. Recent multi-expert
deferral work identifies expert-selection ambiguity and underfitting
\cite{liu2026}; it is a related concern, not an established explanation of our
results. A direct comparison would require a separately specified deferral
setting. We do not claim superiority over those algorithms or state of the art.

\section{Problem formulation and scope}
Each observation comprises eight integer tokens, a $3\times8\times8$ image,
four five-dimensional observed states, and four three-dimensional actions.
The target $y\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ is a synthetic latent class. Domain metadata is
sampled independently of the target; all four transitions are allowed and the
supplied risk score is zero. No corruption identity or target is supplied to
the gate. Three development groups describe clean, familiar-corruption, and
known-shift observations. Group labels enter the objective only.

Five components produce class probabilities $p_j\in\Delta^3$ and states
$z_j\in\mathbb R^{16}$. For two separately initialized banks trained on parity
halves of a bank-fitting partition, we average corresponding probabilities
and states. Thus $p_j=(p_j^{(0)}+p_j^{(1)})/2$ and similarly for $z_j$.
The router receives
\begin{equation}
 x=\operatorname{concat}(z_1,\ldots,z_5,p_1,\ldots,p_5,H(p_1),\ldots,H(p_5))
 \in\mathbb R^{105},\qquad H(p)=-\sum_c p_c\log\max(p_c,10^{-8}).
\end{equation}
These features are detached. Identical bank averaging is used during fitting,
selection, and protected inference. No additional labels, groups, or corruption
metadata are available at inference.

The study concerns four-class classification under this generator. It does not
test language quality, tool use, autonomous research, natural-image reasoning,
deployment safety, or six-family model promotion. The full system contains
auxiliary heads that this objective does not supervise; their existence is not
evidence that they acquired useful capabilities.

\section{Model and chronological training specification}
\subsection{Inherited component banks}
Each bank uses the repository's unchanged reference system. Text branches have
separate weights but share the following architecture. Each text backbone
has vocabulary 32, width 16, one pre-normalized causal attention block, four
attention heads, a GELU feedforward layer of width 32, maximum length eight,
and zero dropout. Token and positional embeddings are added; attention and
feedforward sublayers have residual connections and a final layer normalization.
Hermes uses this text representation. Prometheus adds domain-specific residual
adapters of bottleneck width eight across three domains, with supplied domain
IDs determining adapter selection. Perseus has four width-32 SiLU experts and
top-two routing. Atlas combines text with $2\times2$ image patches through
four-head cross-attention and maps its pooled representation to a normalized
12-dimensional retrieval vector. Kronos uses a width-12 GRU on concatenated
five-dimensional states and three-dimensional actions. Its linear next-state
head predicts all four next states. Aion is present in the inherited system
but is not one of the five class experts.

Last-token Hermes, Prometheus, and Perseus states, the Atlas retrieval vector,
and the last Kronos hidden state each pass through a linear projection and
LayerNorm to width 16. Five separate $16\to4$ heads produce logits. For each
bank, 250 updates optimize
\begin{equation}
 L_{\rm bank}=\frac1{5B}\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{CE}(\ell_{ij},y_i)
 +0.25\,\operatorname{mean}_{i,t,k}(\hat s_{i,t+1,k}-s^*_{i,t+1,k})^2.
\end{equation}
There is no counterfactual-consistency penalty. Each update samples 64 examples
with replacement from that bank's 512-example parity half. The two banks are
then frozen. The exact inherited operators and configuration are distributed
with the source; no external model weights are used.

\subsection{Reference fitting and selection}
The semantic gate is $w(x)=\operatorname{softmax}(W_2\operatorname{GELU}
(W_1\operatorname{LN}(x)+b_1)+b_2)$, with widths $105\to32\to5$.
Its prediction is $p_{\rm sem}=\sum_jw_jp_j$. A fixed gate learns five logits,
initialized at zero. On a separate reference-fitting partition, both receive
300 updates minimizing the sum of their mixture NLLs. Neither loss trains the
frozen banks. Uniform averaging and each single expert are additional choices.
An independent reference-selection partition chooses the highest minimum
group accuracy, then the highest mean group accuracy, then earliest declared
candidate order. The eight candidates are uniform, fixed, semantic, and experts
0 through 4. The resulting $p_0$ is frozen before correction fitting.

\subsection{Alternative, intervention, and loss}
Each alternative starts from a copy of the semantic reference network with
gradients explicitly enabled. Let $q_\theta$ denote its mixture. The final
prediction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blend}
 p_\theta(x)=(1-\alpha_\theta(x))p_0(x)+\alpha_\theta(x)q_\theta(x).
\end{equation}
Direct controls fix $\alpha=1$. Constant controls learn a scalar logit.
Adaptive controls use $\alpha=\sigma(a^Tx+b)$ with $a=0$ and
$b=\log(0.1/0.9)$ initially. Constant controls also initialize at $0.1$.
The alternative network has 3,767 parameters, with zero, one, or 106 additional
intervention parameters. This is a transparent capacity difference, not a
perfectly parameter-matched comparison.

For group $g$, $L_g$ is mean $-\log\max(p_{\theta,y},10^{-8})$ over 32 sampled
examples. The ERM objective averages $L_1,L_2,L_3$; the robust objective takes
their maximum. Constant and adaptive penalized variants add
$0.01\operatorname{mean}(\alpha)$. The seven variants are direct ERM, direct
robust, adaptive ERM, adaptive robust, constant ERM, constant robust, and
adaptive robust without penalty. All receive 300 updates using the same
per-group sample sequence on the correction-fitting partition. Adaptive robust
is the prospectively fixed primary candidate.

All optimization uses AdamW, learning rate $0.001$, weight decay $0.01$, default
Adam moments $(0.9,0.999)$ and $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, and global gradient norm
clipping at one. Nonfinite losses, admitted gradients, or parameters terminate
the run. Shared random seeds fix initialization and sampling. Inference is
argmax over four class probabilities, with the framework's first-index tie
rule. Probabilities must be finite, nonnegative, and sum to one within absolute
tolerance $10^{-5}$; intervention must lie in $[0,1]$.

\subsection{What anchoring does and does not prove}
For any fixed example, equation~\eqref{eq:blend} gives
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(p_\theta,p_0)=\alpha\operatorname{TV}(q_\theta,p_0)\leq\alpha.
\end{equation}
This follows by factoring the nonnegative scalar out of the $\ell_1$ norm.
It bounds distribution movement, not error: a small change can flip a narrow
classification margin. A penalized mean intervention is not a hard per-example
constraint. If $R$ counts wrong-reference/correct-candidate cases and $H$ counts
correct-reference/wrong-candidate cases, then exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rescue}
 \operatorname{Acc}(p_\theta)-\operatorname{Acc}(p_0)=(R-H)/N.
\end{equation}
Useful intervention requires $R>H$. There is no theorem that training NLL,
worst-group loss, or the penalty produces that inequality under shift.

\subsection{Inference cost}
All arms use the same two banks. A routing network costs
$O(105\cdot32+32\cdot5+5\cdot4)$ operations per example, plus normalization
and activations, per routing network. An anchored arm with a semantic reference
evaluates both reference and alternative routing networks. Adaptive intervention
adds one 105-dimensional dot product. The banks are expected to dominate, but
no component-level timing was measured. The experiment retains all variants
for comparison; a deployed single arm would not require all seven. We report
observed runtime without claiming a general scaling law or measured FLOPs.

\section{Data generation, splits, and perturbations}
Labels are uniform across four classes. Initial clean state is independent
Gaussian noise of standard deviation $0.15$ with $1.5$ added at the label
coordinate. Actions are uniform on $[-1,1]^3$. Clean dynamics are
\begin{equation}
 s^*_{t+1}=0.82s^*_t+0.18\tanh(\operatorname{roll}(s^*_t,1))
 +0.12\operatorname{pad}(a_t,2).
\end{equation}
Observed states add Gaussian noise with standard deviation $0.12$. Tokens start
uniformly in $\{8,\ldots,30\}$, with the first three replaced by
$y+1$, $5+(y\bmod2)$, and $7+\lfloor y/2\rfloor$. Images have uniform background
noise below $0.15$, with $0.75$ added to a label-dependent quadrant and channel.
These deliberately redundant class signals make this a mechanism diagnostic.

Broad bank training uniformly samples four branches: clean, one missing
modality, one damaged modality, or one missing plus a different damaged
modality. Missing means zero. Familiar damage randomizes the first three
tokens, replaces the image by uniform noise, or replaces states by Gaussian
noise. For half the broad examples selected independently, damaged observations
instead use reversed first-three tokens, image inversion, or a $1.5$ state bias.
The clean development group has no imposed missingness. Corrupted and known-shift
groups each have one missing and one distinct damaged modality; known shift
uses reversal, inversion, or bias.

The protected composite-shift configuration uses the same missing/damaged
assignment, but damaged text is rolled by two tokens, damaged images have
alternating rows replaced by $0.5$, and damaged states become
$0.5s+\operatorname{linspace}(-0.75,0.75,4)$. Only the designated damaged
modality gets its transform. These perturbation types were explored historically;
their reserved configuration is not a previously unknown mechanism or natural
data OOD. Targets and source IDs are unchanged by observation transformations.

Five dataset seeds are 20267100, 20267200, 20267300, 20267400, and 20267500.
Each is repeated with training seeds 20267601 and 20267602. Bank initialization
uses training seed plus $100b$ for bank $b$; bank sampling adds 10000.
Reference sampling adds one. Correction construction resets the RNG to the
training seed plus two, but alternative weights are copied from the trained
semantic reference and intervention parameters are set to the stated constants.
Correction sampling adds three. The second optimizer seed shares each dataset's examples
and is not a sixth independent dataset.

\section{Frozen experimental methodology}
\subsection{Controls and selection}
After correction fitting, the separate final-selection partition chooses among
uniform, fixed, semantic, direct ERM, direct robust, constant robust, constant
ERM, and experts 0--4, in that order. Selection maximizes worst-group accuracy,
then mean accuracy, then declared order. This selected control is fixed before
protected evaluation. It is not the best control chosen after seeing test
results. Every direct control receives the extra 300 updates, making additional
training an explicit competing explanation. Adaptive ERM separates robust
training from anchoring; constant controls separate input dependence from
shrinkage; the no-penalty arm tests the regularizer.

\subsection{Primary rule and uncertainty}
The primary contrast is adaptive robust minus selected control accuracy on
composite shift. Support requires mean gain at least $0.02$ and descriptive
95\% interval lower bound strictly above zero. For each clean, corrupted, and
known-shift regime, nonregression against both selected control and reference
requires mean delta at least $-0.01$ and interval lower bound at least $-0.02$.
All seven criteria must hold. A primary failure is retained regardless of any
favorable secondary metric or arm.

For each dataset, the two optimizer-seed paired deltas are averaged. The five
dataset deltas are then resampled with replacement 10,000 times using NumPy's
default generator seeded 20267999. The mean of five sampled deltas gives each
bootstrap replicate; percentile 2.5 and 97.5 bounds are descriptive pilot
intervals. Five clusters are too few for strong general population claims.
All per-dataset deltas are reported. Secondary comparisons are exploratory,
without multiplicity-adjusted significance declarations.

Metrics include accuracy, NLL, summed-class Brier score, ten-bin expected
calibration error, top-half confidence selective accuracy, intervention amount,
prediction changes, rescues, and harms. We retain individual expert predictions.
The rescue identity is checked arithmetically. Primary and nonregression rules
are fixed before execution; no protected result may select a new primary.

\subsection{Execution, freeze, and failure policy}
One campaign contains ten development cells, twenty bank fits, seven correction
fits per cell, and forty protected reports. There are zero scientific
development revisions, zero paid jobs, and no external data or weight downloads.
The environment is a locked CPU installation with one Torch thread and
deterministic algorithms. A 120-second artificial qualification must pass on
the exact scientific source. The campaign's hard subprocess deadline is
3,600 seconds. Resource checks require at least 2 GiB available memory and at
most 90\% swap use; macOS additionally requires 512 MiB raw free memory and
stable pageouts. Failures stop and retain partial artifacts.

Before generating protected data, the freeze binds the protocol, source hashes,
Python/Torch/platform contract, all development file identities, reference and
control selections, checkpoints, and split hashes. Qualification tests include
convex endpoints, gradients after freezing a reference, hand-calculated group
losses, disjointness, tie-breaking, complete tiny lifecycle, and deliberate
tampering. Verification regenerates every protected split, reloads both banks and all
routers, exactly replays probabilities and intervention, and recomputes metrics
and summaries. During development, checkpoint reload separately regenerates
final-selection features and compares predictions exactly. Other development
partitions retain IDs and tensor hashes; verification does not replay their
training trajectories. This is computational replay, not independent retraining.

\subsection{Prospective limitations and falsifiers}
A positive result would support only this bounded synthetic configuration.
Correlated experts may leave little corrective opportunity; the selected
reference may already be strong; the intervention penalty may suppress useful
changes; and worst-group optimization can fit the wrong training surrogate.
Our ablations distinguish some of these alternatives but cannot establish a
complete causal explanation. No model/data-scale sweep, broad hyperparameter
sensitivity study, or external benchmark is admitted. Such work requires a
successor protocol rather than post-hoc expansion of this test. Invalid
provenance or numerical behavior invalidates the test; absence of execution
means not evaluated, not negative performance.
