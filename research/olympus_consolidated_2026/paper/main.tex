\title{Simple Controls Remain Hard to Beat in Learned Multimodal Routing:\\
\large Five Controlled Studies and a Supported Residual Dynamics Result}
\author{Ryan\\Independent Researcher}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Adaptive fusion is often motivated by the intuition that a learned router should
down-weight unreliable inputs better than a fixed mixture. We tested this intuition in a
sequence of prospectively specified studies built around Olympus, an auditable reference
architecture for heterogeneous prediction. Four synthetic studies progressively added
explicit reliability supervision, independently trained experts, cross-fitted routing,
broader corruption coverage, and development-only route selection. A fifth study used the
licensed EditCLEVR paired-image benchmark. Across all five studies, the registered learned
routing candidate did not beat its strongest matched simple control on the primary
endpoint. The successive primary effects were $-0.0469$ selective-accuracy points versus
uniform fusion, $-0.0125$ versus semantic routing, $-0.0682$ accuracy points versus
semantic routing, $-0.00068$ versus semantic routing on novel shift, and $-0.00005$
versus a development-selected EditCLEVR control. In contrast, a separately registered
residual temporal predictor reduced clean mean-squared error from $0.07133$ for the prior
predictor to $0.01457$, beat persistence by $0.01540$ with a paired descriptive 95\%
interval of $[-0.01583,-0.01492]$, and retained corrupted-input recovery. The evidence
supports mixed-corruption training, sample-dependent semantic routing in one observed
corruption regime, and residual prediction for the synthetic dynamics task. It does not
support learned routing as a general improvement, natural-data robustness, deployment,
safety efficacy, or a full cognitive architecture. The contribution is a preserved
sequence of falsifiable iterations showing where increasingly elaborate routing repairs
failed, where one simpler architectural prior succeeded, and how negative outcomes can be
reported without retroactive endpoint selection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multimodal prediction systems must operate when some inputs are missing, corrupted, or
less useful for the current example. Deep fusion can obscure which input drove a decision,
while mixture-of-experts systems add their own gate-collapse and optimization failure
modes \cite{fedus2021switch,zhou2022expertchoice,wang2024lossfree}. Missing-modality
studies also show that robustness is sensitive to the dataset, missingness pattern, and
fusion design \cite{ma2022missing,maheshwari2023m3l}. Recent work has therefore explored
explicit routing, reliability estimation, reconstruction, and sequential evidence
selection \cite{hooman2026routing,kumar2026prime,wang2026metamodal}.

The motivating hypothesis is attractive: if a router can infer input reliability for each
example, it should outperform a uniform or fixed mixture under shift. The hypothesis is
also easy to make unfalsifiable. A router can be changed after inspecting test outcomes;
an oracle can be mistaken for a deployable method; a weak baseline can make any adaptive
mechanism look useful; or an overall score can hide a clean-versus-shift tradeoff. This
paper instead reports a sequence in which every failed primary outcome remained terminal
for its registered protocol and every repair became a separately versioned successor.

Our main findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Five learned-routing candidates failed their primary comparison, including a test
  on the public, CC-BY-4.0 EditCLEVR benchmark \cite{karnam2026editclevr}.
  \item The failures persisted after the experiments added direct reliability labels,
  independently supervised expert banks, cross-fitting, corruption-diverse development
  data, and development-only selection among candidate routers.
  \item Mixed-corruption training and semantic routing nevertheless produced large matched
  improvements in one synthetic corruption regime. These are narrower findings than a
  general routing claim and did not transfer uniformly to held-out shifts.
  \item A residual temporal predictor passed every registered primary criterion, including
  a paired comparison with persistence. This positive result concerns synthetic dynamics,
  not the routing hypothesis or the full Olympus system.
\end{enumerate}

The study is deliberately not presented as state of the art. The models are small, four
studies are synthetic, the external study uses one benchmark, and the final two remote
evidence archives were verified in their executing notebook but were not recovered into
the repository. Those constraints are part of the result, not footnotes to be removed.

\section{Architecture and claim boundary}
Olympus is a reference architecture in which $m$ component families emit states $h_i$.
Each state is projected and normalized,
\begin{align}
z_i &= \operatorname{LayerNorm}(W_i h_i+b_i), \\
g &= \softmax(r([z_1;\ldots;z_m])/\tau), \\
z &= \operatorname{LayerNorm}\left(\sum_i g_i z_i\right),
\end{align}
and a shared decision head acts on $z$. The system also exposes leave-one-component-out
states
\begin{equation}
z_{-i}=\operatorname{LayerNorm}\left(
\frac{\sum_j g_jz_j-g_iz_i}{\max(1-g_i,\epsilon)}\right),
\end{equation}
so that component-removal behavior remains observable. A counterfactual regularizer can
penalize divergence between the full and removal predictions, with the full prediction
stop-gradient treated as the target.

The architecture distinguishes learned intrinsic risk $q$ from a host-provided risk floor
$r_h$. Their effective value is
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{eff}}=1-(1-r_h)(1-q).
\end{equation}
For $q,r_h\in[0,1]$, $r_{\mathrm{eff}}-r_h=q(1-r_h)\geq0$. Learned evidence therefore
cannot reduce the host risk floor. Host-provided legal-transition masks are applied after
the learned scores, and a fixed threshold can force STOP. These are algebraic and software
invariants. They are not evidence that the learned risk is calibrated, that the host risk
is correct, or that deployment is safe. Selective prediction and conformal risk control
provide important neighboring frameworks, but the invariant alone supplies neither
coverage nor distribution-free risk control \cite{geifman2019selectivenet,
angelopoulos2022crc}.

The repository names six component families, but the studies below isolate routing and
temporal mechanisms at reference scale. They do not validate six-family joint capability,
the separately specified joint-embedding upscaler, production behavior, or any cognitive
claim.

\section{Sequential research design}
Each iteration was opened only after the predecessor result had been recorded. Protocols
fixed the question, controls, source-disjoint splits, budgets, endpoint, effect threshold,
and protected-outcome chronology before confirmation. A failed study could motivate a new
hypothesis, but its endpoint, data, and disposition were not rewritten.

All paired intervals reported below are fixed-seed percentile bootstrap intervals over
five training seeds or five independently generated dataset clusters, depending on the
protocol. With only five units, they are descriptive rather than asymptotically precise.
Secondary outcomes cannot rescue a failed primary endpoint. Oracle routes are diagnostics
that use privileged ground truth and are never counted as deployable candidates.

\subsection{v2: controlled reliability shift}
The first confirmatory study generated 1,408 source-disjoint units: 512 training, 128
development, and three protected sets of 256 for clean, corrupted, and out-of-distribution
conditions. Five matched variants were trained for five seeds, giving 25 fits and 75
protected evaluations. The primary claim required the full mechanism to exceed uniform
fusion by at least 0.02 clean accuracy and 0.03 corrupted selective accuracy, with positive
paired interval lower bounds.

\subsection{v3: direct reliability supervision}
The successor asked whether reliability identification could repair routing. It added one
reliability head per component, supervised those heads using known interventions, combined
semantic and reliability evidence in log space, and shrank the result toward uniform.
Reliability targets were labels during training and were not supplied at inference. Five
matched variants, five seeds, 25 fits, and 75 protected evaluations were frozen. The
primary endpoint was corrupted selective accuracy at 50\% coverage against semantic
routing, with a required effect of at least 0.02 and a positive interval lower bound.

\subsection{v4: expert-bank diagnostic}
The third iteration removed label-recoverable metadata shortcuts and introduced five
independently supervised expert heads. Two-fold cross-fitting ensured that routing features
for an example came from a bank not trained on that example. Missing-only and mixed
missing-plus-perturbed training were paired on identical latent examples and budgets.
Utility, semantic, fixed, uniform, corruption-proxy, best-single, and privileged oracle
routes shared the same banks. The design contained 40 cells, 80 expert-bank fits, and 120
protected regime evaluations. The utility router had to beat the development-selected
control by at least 0.02 on corrupted accuracy with a positive cluster interval lower
bound.

\subsection{v5: broader shifts and residual dynamics}
The fourth synthetic iteration used fresh units and reserved a genuinely novel synthetic
shift family for protected evaluation. Core training used the v4 corruption set. Broad
training added token reversal, image inversion, and positive state bias during development;
novel protected shifts used token relocation, checkerboard image occlusion, and negative
time-varying state drift. Five dataset seeds and two training seeds yielded 20 cells, 40
bank fits, and 80 protected evaluations.

Candidate routing was selected using development clean, corrupted, and known-shift data by
maximizing worst-regime and then mean accuracy under a fixed tie order. The registered
routing comparison was broad selected routing minus core semantic routing on novel shift,
with a required effect of 0.02 and corrupted non-regression.

The same protocol separately compared a prior temporal predictor, persistence, a direct
MLP, and a residual MLP. The two new MLPs had the same $88\mathord{\to}64\mathord{\to}5$
architecture, initialization, 500-update budget, and inputs. The residual model alone
added the observed state to the predicted change. It had to reduce clean MSE by at least
10\% relative to both the prior predictor and persistence, have a residual-minus-
persistence interval upper bound below zero, and remain within 0.02 MSE of the prior model
under corruption.

\subsection{EditCLEVR paired-image study}
EditCLEVR provides before-and-after CLEVR-style scenes with known object-factor
interventions and controlled distribution shifts \cite{karnam2026editclevr}. We pinned
dataset revision \texttt{734efa9b0164ba742cd2c39b9c2945c494497c23}, whose local receipt
accounts for 20,000 unique pair identifiers and 120,000 referenced files with no missing
paths. The dataset is CC-BY-4.0.

The bounded study predicts the edit factor rather than claiming the benchmark's full
scene-graph intervention metric. Shared convolutional encoders feed before-image,
after-image, absolute-change, and paired-image experts. Learned, uniform, pair-only, and
delta-only predictions come from the same checkpoint. The strongest simple control is
selected on validation accuracy. Five optimizer seeds ran 15 epochs each; four protected
splits produced 20 protected reports. The primary effect averages hard and CoGenT-shifted
accuracy. It required a learned-minus-control mean of at least 0.02 with a positive
interval lower bound, plus in-distribution non-regression.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Learned routing did not win a primary comparison}

effect threshold, and four point estimates favored the matched control. The EditCLEVR
effect was effectively a tie: four seeds tied exactly and one was slightly worse.

The v2 full mechanism tied uniform fusion on clean accuracy exactly, but its corrupted
selective point estimate was worse. Its gates had normalized entropy 0.922 and an effective
component count of 4.42, so hard gate collapse was not the main explanation. Intrinsic risk
was weakly aligned with the constructed corruption target, yielding a high risk Brier score
of 0.741.

In v3, reliability supervision produced positive mean separation between intact and
damaged positions under corruption, but that signal did not improve the task endpoint.
This distinguishes failure to identify corruption from failure to convert a coarse
reliability signal into useful task routing. A source can be damaged yet remain
discriminative, or remain clean while being redundant.

In v4, the utility router lost to semantic routing in all five dataset clusters. Privileged
diagnostics showed that useful expert signal still existed: on corrupted inputs, a task
oracle exceeded uniform fusion by 0.1895 and a corruption oracle exceeded it by 0.1482.
Those oracles use unavailable ground truth, so they identify headroom without constituting
a deployable result.

In v5, broader coverage plus development-only candidate selection did not improve novel
shift performance over the core semantic control, and it materially regressed on the
original corruption regime by $-0.05869$ with interval $[-0.06387,-0.05068]$. This rejects
the idea that the registered selection rule converted broader observed shifts into robust
router choice.

On EditCLEVR, the learned router tied its selected control in four of five seeds and was
worse by 0.00025 in one. Its mean in-distribution difference was $-0.000067$ with interval
$[-0.00020,0]$, which passed the registered non-regression condition but supplied no
evidence of improvement.

\subsection{Narrow secondary improvements}
The failed primary routing claim does not imply that every intervention was useless. In
v4, mixed missing-plus-perturbed training improved semantic-router corrupted accuracy from
68.32\% to 95.74\%, a matched increase of 27.42 percentage points with interval
$[25.30,29.28]$. On the same mixed-training banks, semantic routing exceeded learned fixed
routing by 10.21 points, interval $[9.62,10.76]$, and its own mean training gates by 11.54
points, interval $[10.27,13.18]$. These outcomes show that input-dependent routing can help
within the observed corruption regime.

The gain did not generalize uniformly. Semantic routing scored 91.99\% on the v4 OOD split,
below uniform at 97.88\%, learned fixed at 98.64\%, and the utility router at 99.55\%.
Selecting a post hoc hybrid after observing these protected outcomes would create a new
hypothesis rather than repair the v4 result.

\subsection{Invariant and denominator checks}
The v2 and v3 studies each completed 25 fits and 75 protected evaluations. v4 completed 40
cells, 80 bank fits, and 120 protected evaluations. v5 completed 20 cells, 40 bank fits,
and 80 protected evaluations. EditCLEVR completed five training seeds and 20 protected
reports. No failed or unfavorable study was deleted from the sequence. The v2 and v3
protected receipts reported zero host-risk-monotonicity and transition-legality violations.
These counts establish completeness for the stated study plans, not external replication.

\section{Why increasingly elaborate routing repairs failed}
The sequence narrows several plausible explanations.

\paragraph{Reliability is not utility.}
v3 learned a corruption-related signal without improving task routing. Reliability labels
describe an intervention, while the desired gate depends on class-conditional information,
redundancy, calibration, and interactions among experts.

\paragraph{Useful experts do not imply a learnable router.}
The v4 task oracle revealed substantial headroom. Its access to true-class likelihood is
exactly what a deployed router lacks. Cross-fitting removed direct in-sample leakage but did
not make the utility target identify the correct expert under shift.

\paragraph{Coverage can trade one shift for another.}
Mixed-corruption training strongly repaired the observed corruption regime, yet semantic
routing regressed on v4 OOD inputs. Broader v5 development coverage still did not improve
the reserved novel shifts and harmed the original corruption comparison.

\paragraph{Selection adds another generalization problem.}
Choosing among routers on development data does not guarantee that the selected rule will
transfer to a new intervention family. v5 froze selection before protected access, making
that failure visible instead of silently selecting the protected winner.

\paragraph{Simple inductive bias can outperform learned arbitration.}
The residual temporal model embeds a direct assumption: predict change around the observed
state. On the frozen dynamics task, this bias was more effective than adding another learned
selection mechanism. This does not make residual prediction universally superior; it shows
that a matched simple baseline should be treated as a first-class candidate.

\section{Evidence integrity and reproducibility}
The repository at
\url{https://github.com/build-the-future-11/olympus-cognitive-architecture}
contains the implementation, protocols, frozen configuration identities, historical
states, tests, and manuscript source. Earlier v2, v3, and v4 evidence was recovered locally
with source and prediction hashes. The v5 and EditCLEVR executing notebooks produced
complete verifier reports: 80 and 20 prediction hashes respectively, recomputed aggregate
means and paired deltas, checkpoint/source chronology checks, and zero cross-fit overlap
where applicable. These verifiers establish provenance and arithmetic consistency, not an
independent retraining.

\section{Limitations}
Four routing studies use synthetic generators and small reference models. Synthetic
interventions provide exact labels and controlled pairing but cannot represent the full
dependence structure of natural multimodal data. EditCLEVR is public and licensed, but our
study predicts edit factors and does not execute the benchmark's complete object-centric
faithfulness protocol. Five seeds or five dataset clusters offer limited precision, and the
intervals are descriptive. The same research program designed and verified the studies;
there is no independent operator reproduction. The final remote archives are not locally
available. No target-scale model, human evaluation, energy study, calibrated deployment,
rights-reviewed six-family corpus, or safety intervention is evaluated. The clean synthetic
classification tasks often saturate, which limits their ability to distinguish methods.

The result also does not establish that all learned routing must fail. It rejects five
specific, frozen mechanisms under their stated data and budgets. Other targets, causal
signals, architectures, or substantially larger datasets may behave differently. Testing
them would require fresh hypotheses and protected examples rather than reuse of these
outcomes for tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
Across five controlled studies, adding reliability supervision, expert-bank separation,
cross-fitting, corruption coverage, and development-only route selection did not make the
registered learned router outperform its strongest simple matched control. The sequence
does support two narrower conclusions: corruption-diverse training can matter greatly for
the corruption families it covers, and input-dependent semantic routing can outperform a
fixed mixture within one such regime. Neither finding generalized into a successful
reserved-shift routing primary.

The residual temporal predictor provides a contrasting positive result. It passed a
predeclared comparison with both the prior predictor and persistence while preserving
corrupted-input performance. The practical lesson is not that complexity never helps. It
is that adaptive routing, reliability estimation, and model selection each create a new
generalization problem, whereas a strong simple control can encode the right structure
directly. Preserving every failed iteration made that conclusion possible.
