\begin{abstract}
Research-agent projects often accumulate branded ``models'' before they have
datasets, checkpoints, interfaces, or falsifiable evaluations. We present a
counterproposal for six planned Olympus families: Hermes for grounded response,
Prometheus for scientific synthesis, Perseus for tool execution,
\atlas{} for retrieval over governed corpora, Kronos for temporal planning and
guarded adaptation, and Aion for governed research loops. The families share a
revision-pinned decoder, typed workspace, immutable evidence graph, and
grammar-constrained output envelopes; they differ through adapters, auxiliary
heads, services, and promotion criteria. Deterministic systems retain access
control, permission checks, transactions, protocol freezing, audit, rollback,
and promotion. We specify module boundaries, objectives, data requirements,
threat models, matched-budget baselines, ablations, and rejection rules for each
family. We also argue that Aion should begin as a deterministic controller, not
a monolithic autonomous-scientist model, and that its learned router should be
removed unless it beats that controller at equal budget and false-promotion
rate. This manuscript is an architecture and evaluation protocol. It reports
\emph{no trained family checkpoint and no family-level experimental result}.
Its contribution is therefore a falsifiable research plan rather than a claim
of model capability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Language models can retrieve external knowledge, invoke tools, explore multiple
reasoning paths, model trajectories, and participate in automated research
loops. These ingredients do not automatically compose into a reliable research
agent. Retrieval can return irrelevant or unauthorized evidence; syntactically
valid tool calls can be semantically wrong; multiple reasoning traces can agree
on the same defect; online adaptation can erase prior capabilities; and a model
that proposes a protocol cannot be allowed to approve its own deviations.

The Olympus repository contains real lifecycle infrastructure and small
verification models, but its named model families remain specifications. In
particular, the planned Hermes base experiment has not produced a qualifying
checkpoint, and Prometheus, Perseus, Atlas, Kronos, and Aion have no qualifying
datasets or evaluations. We preserve that boundary. The question addressed by
this paper is not ``how capable are these models?'' but ``what is the smallest
coherent architecture that could test whether the proposed specializations add
value?''

Our central decision is that six roles do not justify six foundation-model
pretraining programs. We instead propose one shared substrate with independently
versioned adapters and services. This yields four contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item a typed contract separating language proposals from authorized state
transitions;
\item concrete architectures for six roles, including family-specific data,
losses, interfaces, baselines, failure modes, and stop rules;
\item a dependency and promotion protocol that prevents downstream branding
from substituting for upstream evidence; and
\item a paper-level claim ledger that labels every design as proposed and every
future numerical claim as inadmissible without immutable run evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The proposal is deliberately scale-skeptical. The first compatible base is the
already selected Qwen3-0.6B-Base candidate; Qwen3 reports dense and mixture-of-
experts variants starting at the 0.6B scale \citep{yang2025qwen3}. This citation
supports a compatibility decision, not a transfer of reported Qwen3 results to
Olympus. A larger base is considered only after data quality and system-level
evaluation are demonstrated at the cheapest credible scale.

\section{Related architectural evidence}

\paragraph{Backbone.} The Transformer supplies the common sequence-modeling
substrate \citep{vaswani2017attention}. Rotary position embeddings and
grouped-query attention provide relative-position and inference-efficiency
mechanisms \citep{su2021roformer,ainslie2023gqa}. Olympus inherits these from
the candidate backbone; it does not claim to revalidate them.

\paragraph{Retrieval and memory.} Retrieval-augmented generation and RETRO show
ways to combine parametric sequence models with external corpora
\citep{lewis2020rag,borgeaud2021retro}. ColBERT provides an efficient late-
interaction ranking design \citep{khattab2020colbert}. However, long-context
models can be position-sensitive, with relevant information in the middle used
less reliably \citep{liu2023lost}. MemGPT demonstrates an operating-system-
inspired hierarchy for moving information through limited context
\citep{packer2023memgpt}. These results motivate explicit retrieval, memory
tiers, and position-aware tests; they do not make generated citations faithful
or memory writes trustworthy by default.

\paragraph{Reasoning, verification, and action.} ReAct interleaves reasoning
and acting, while Toolformer trains language models to invoke APIs
\citep{yao2022react,schick2023toolformer}. PICARD shows that incremental parsing
can constrain outputs in a formal language \citep{scholak2021picard}.
Self-consistency and Tree of Thoughts explore multiple inference paths
\citep{wang2022selfconsistency,yao2023tot}; process supervision provides a basis
for scoring intermediate steps \citep{lightman2023verify}. A separate line of
work studies whether models can estimate correctness and knowledge
\citep{kadavath2022know}. We take the conservative intersection: expose
checkable states, constrain formal actions, measure calibration, and never
equate model confidence with authority.

\paragraph{Temporal learning and agents.} Decision Transformer formulates
offline decision making as conditional sequence modeling
\citep{chen2021decision}; Mamba provides a selective state-space alternative
for long sequences \citep{gu2023mamba}. Elastic weight consolidation is a
classic regularization approach to catastrophic forgetting
\citep{kirkpatrick2016ewc}. Each is an ablation candidate for Kronos, not a
preselected winner. AgentBench, $\tau$-bench, and ToolSandbox emphasize
interactive and stateful evaluation
\citep{liu2023agentbench,yao2024taubench,lu2024toolsandbox}. Constitutional AI studies supervision from explicit
principles \citep{bai2022constitutional}, while The AI Scientist demonstrates
the active direction of end-to-end research automation
\citep{lu2024aiscientist}. Olympus differs by keeping authorization, audit, and
promotion outside the learned policy.

\section{Shared evidence-and-action substrate}

\subsection{Backbone and specialization}

Let $B_\theta$ be a revision-pinned decoder-only Transformer and $A_f$ a
family-specific adapter. For family $f$, the learned policy is
\begin{equation}
p_f(y\mid x)=B_{\theta,A_f}(x), \qquad \theta\ \text{frozen in the first stage}.
\end{equation}
Adapters target attention and feed-forward projections. Auxiliary heads may
read final hidden states for evidence entailment, confidence, outcome value, or
policy-violation prediction. Retrieval encoders and process verifiers may be
separate small bidirectional models where independence or latency matters.

The first implementation supplies retrieved evidence as prefix tokens rather
than adding cross-attention. This choice minimizes surgery to the base and makes
``retrieval quality'' separable from ``decoder use of retrieved evidence.'' A
cross-attention variant is eligible only if the prefix baseline saturates.

\subsection{Workspace state}

Every family reads a serialized \texttt{WorkspaceState}:
\begin{equation}
W=(o,c,b,E,C,G,T,P,H,M),
\end{equation}
where $o$ is the objective, $c$ constraints, $b$ the remaining resource budget,
$E$ immutable evidence references, $C$ claims, $G$ a plan DAG, $T$ tool schemas,
$P$ permissions and approvals, $H$ observation history, and $M$ exact model and
environment identities. Evidence references include content hash, source URI,
acquisition time, license, access label, and span offsets. Claims have explicit
support, refute, and unknown edges.

The model emits exactly one grammar-constrained envelope:

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{Answer\{claims,citations,confidence,abstention?\}}\\
\texttt{Proposal\{hypotheses,tests,assumptions,confidence\}}\\
\texttt{Action\{tool,schema,args,preconditions,idempotency\}}\\
\texttt{Stop\{reason,unresolved,required\_authority?\}}.
\end{quote}

No family writes directly to external state. A deterministic validator parses
the envelope, checks access and permissions, executes a permitted transition,
and appends an observation. Untrusted tool text cannot modify policy fields.
Confidence is an evaluated prediction, never an authorization mechanism.

\subsection{Learning objective and evidence protocol}

For a family $f$, the general objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_f=\mathcal{L}_{LM}+\lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{schema}
+\lambda_a\mathcal{L}_{attribution}+\lambda_c\mathcal{L}_{calibration}
+\lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{policy}+\mathcal{L}^{specific}_f.
\end{equation}
Loss weights and stopping rules are frozen before sequestered evaluation. Each
component requires an ablation; otherwise the architecture cannot establish
which mechanism mattered. Training records must be license-cleared,
deduplicated, PII-screened, manifest-bound, and split by source or environment
rather than adjacent rows. Teacher outputs carry teacher revision and rights
metadata and cannot enter held-out sets.

Results are reported at three levels: component metrics, end-to-end final state,
and safety/system integrity. Every experiment retains per-example outputs,
dataset and code hashes, checkpoint identity, seeds, latency, peak memory,
tokens, tool calls, failures, and confidence intervals. Required baselines are
the unadapted base, a deterministic non-LLM system, a single shared adapter, and
the proposed specialization under matched budgets.

\section{Family architectures}

\subsection{Hermes: grounded interpretation and response}

Hermes is the only user-facing answer generator. Its pipeline is request
interpretation $\rightarrow$ authorized retrieval $\rightarrow$ context
composition $\rightarrow$ grounded decoding $\rightarrow$ attribution and
confidence validation. A request adapter decomposes compound intents and emits
retrieval queries. Retrieved passages retain source IDs beside their spans and
are packed under a fixed token budget. The decoder produces an
\texttt{Answer}; an entailment head scores each claim/evidence pair as support,
refute, or insufficient; a confidence head predicts answer correctness and
evidence sufficiency. A memory selector can only \emph{propose} a write. A
separate ACL and provenance service decides whether it persists.

Hermes training requires licensed question/evidence/answer triples,
contradictions, answerable and unanswerable cases, citation spans,
memory-consent examples, and prompt-injection negatives. Its additional losses
cover claim attribution, abstention, Brier-style confidence, and memory-write
selection. Splits are by document and source. Evaluation reports answer
correctness, citation precision/recall, entailment, expected calibration error,
Brier score, risk--coverage, unsupported-claim rate, memory leakage, latency,
and peak memory.

Baselines are no retrieval, BM25 retrieval, and the unadapted base with the same
context. The family is rejected if gains vanish after document-level
decontamination or if more citation markers do not increase supported claims.
Promotion also requires existing Foundry gates for held-out scale,
quantization, fresh-process serving, license, safety, and model cards.

\subsection{Prometheus: scientific synthesis by proposer and verifier}

Prometheus formalizes a question into variables, assumptions, candidate
hypotheses, evidence gaps, and falsification tests. A proposer adapter samples a
bounded hypothesis lattice. Branches store compact claims and planned checks,
not unverifiable private prose. Calculators, code sandboxes, proof checkers, and
retrieval attach receipts to branches. A process-verifier adapter scores each
checkable transition; an evidence adjudicator preserves conflicts rather than
forcing consensus. A selector uses verifier score, evidence coverage, branch
diversity, and remaining budget to select or abstain, after which Hermes renders
the user-facing result.

Proposer and verifier initially share frozen backbone weights but use disjoint
adapters and heads. To measure correlated error, training sources and evaluator
authors are split. If correlation remains high, the verifier moves to a
different architecture before the proposer scales. Data include licensed
proofs, executable mathematics, systematic reviews with source spans,
contradictory and retracted claims, negative results, and perturbed invalid
steps. Specific objectives measure branch diversity, process validity,
contradiction recognition, and final outcome.

Matched-compute baselines are greedy decoding, self-consistency, and tree search
without a learned verifier. Outcomes include exact answers, verified-step
precision/recall, citation entailment, contradiction detection, calibration,
abstention, cost, and proposer/verifier error correlation. Reject Prometheus if
equal-compute sampling matches it or if verifier selection favors persuasive
invalid branches over deterministic outcome checks.

\subsection{Perseus: typed tool action and recovery}

Perseus serializes goal, observations, budget, tool schemas, and transaction
status, then emits an \texttt{Action} through incremental grammar-constrained
decoding. A non-learned capability kernel resolves the exact tool and schema,
validates argument types and preconditions, checks permissions and approval,
assigns an idempotency key, and executes in a sandbox. An observation normalizer
records structured outputs and partial effects while isolating untrusted tool
content. A recovery critic classifies failures as retryable, compensatable,
fatal, or authority-required. Material writes require a checkpoint and explicit
commit; retries reuse their idempotency key.

Training traces must contain successes, failures, minimal-tool solutions,
permission denials, stale observations, partial writes, compensating actions,
and tool-output prompt injection. Losses cover tool selection, typed arguments,
preconditions, outcome prediction, recovery, and unsafe attempts. Evaluation
uses resettable stateful environments and compares deterministic workflows,
ReAct prompting, unconstrained function calling, and Perseus without recovery.
Primary metrics are final-state equality, schema validity, tool/argument
accuracy, excess calls, recovery, idempotency, budget, false refusal, unsafe
attempts, and actual side effects. A textual statement of success has zero
weight. Any unauthorized write blocks promotion.

\subsection{Olympus-Atlas: governed retrieval and attribution}

\atlas{} ingests documents into overlapping semantic passages while preserving
document/version hashes, timestamps, license, ACL, and original span offsets.
Access filtering occurs \emph{before} lexical or neural scoring so ranks do not
expose hidden corpus membership. Candidate generation fuses BM25 and a compact
bi-encoder. A ColBERT-style late-interaction model reranks only authorized
candidates. A context composer selects diverse passages under a token budget,
keeps source IDs inline, and varies evidence position. An attribution adapter
generates evidence graphs; a separate entailment head validates edges. Immutable
indexes swap atomically and remain replayable by version.

Training uses query/passage judgments, within-document hard negatives, temporal
replacements, contradictions, ACL canaries, and multi-document questions. It
optimizes contrastive retrieval, late-interaction ranking, freshness selection,
attribution, and abstention. Metrics include Recall@$k$, nDCG@10, answer and
citation correctness, entailment, freshness, evidence-position sensitivity,
index lag, latency, memory, and ACL leakage. Baselines are BM25, dense-only,
long-context without retrieval, hybrid without reranking, and the full system.
Zero ACL leakage is a hard gate. Do not extend context if retrieval remains the
binding error, and reject the neural stack if hybrid search cannot beat BM25 at
matched latency.

\subsection{Kronos: temporal planning and guarded adaptation}

Kronos converts timestamped observations, interventions, delayed outcomes,
missingness, and environment versions into event tokens. A compact selective
state-space encoder summarizes long streams; a Transformer readout can attend
to retrieved raw episodes. The state-space module is explicitly an ablation. A
forecaster emits calibrated outcome distributions at declared horizons. A
return- and constraint-conditioned planner emits a receding-horizon plan DAG
with checkpoints and stop conditions. After Perseus executes an authorized
step, an observer compares predicted and actual state and triggers replanning.

Adaptation occurs offline. A manager trains a new adapter on a deletion-aware
replay set and compares no update, naive fine-tuning, replay, and regularization.
Only the promotion service can atomically swap adapters. Every plan and adapter
records environment, data, base, and predecessor hashes; rollback is tested
before promotion.

Data include timestamped trajectories, interventions, delayed or censored
outcomes, drift episodes, and clearly labeled simulations. Adjacent time points
cannot cross train/test boundaries. Forward-chaining evaluation reports
calibration by horizon, plan success, regret, recovery, adaptation gain,
backward transfer/forgetting, deletion compliance, and rollback identity.
Online weight mutation is forbidden until a predeclared forgetting bound and a
fresh-process rollback test pass.

\subsection{Aion: governed research-loop controller}

Aion is primarily a protocol state machine:
\begin{equation}
\text{question}\rightarrow\text{evidence}\rightarrow\text{hypotheses}
\rightarrow\text{frozen protocol}\rightarrow\text{authorized execution}
\rightarrow\text{audit}\rightarrow\{\text{revise, stop, review}\}.
\end{equation}
Every transition has typed inputs, a deterministic admission predicate, a
budget, an authorized actor, and an immutable receipt. Inspecting outcomes can
never silently modify a frozen protocol; any change creates a post-hoc version.

A deterministic controller and routing table are the required baseline.
Prometheus proposes hypotheses and protocols; \atlas{} maps claims to sources;
Perseus performs bounded execution; Kronos monitors delayed outcomes; and
Pantheon audits canonical packages and evidence identities. Protected test
answers are invisible to proposers. Learned components cannot waive audit or
promotion. A learned router may predict the next permitted service and budget,
but deterministic masks make illegal transitions unreachable. External writes,
publication, high-cost jobs, and evidence promotion require human authority.

The optional router trains only on versioned traces containing negative
results, interruptions, permission denials, and independently reviewed claim
maps. Evaluation measures valid-experiment rate, protocol deviation,
false-promotion rate, claim precision, evidence coverage, protected-data
leakage, budget, intervention recovery, and external reproduction. The decisive
falsification criterion is simple: remove the learned Aion component if a
deterministic controller with identical tools and budget matches valid-
experiment rate at equal or lower false-promotion rate. Self-approval, protocol
rewriting after outcomes, hidden-test access, unbounded recursion, or an
unauthorized effect is a hard failure.

\section{End-to-end dependency and deployment plan}

The dependency order is Foundry $\rightarrow$ Hermes $\rightarrow$
\{Prometheus, Perseus\} $\rightarrow$ \atlas{} $\rightarrow$ Kronos
$\rightarrow$ Aion, with Pantheon auditing every stage. This is a promotion
dependency, not a ban on prototyping interfaces in parallel. Aion can exist
immediately as a deterministic state machine, but no learned autonomous behavior
can be promoted before the relevant upstream services and rollback controls are
real.

Serving loads one quantized backbone and switches signed adapter bundles.
Retrieval, capability, transaction, temporal, and audit services are separate
processes with narrow schemas. This separation costs some latency but preserves
ownership: a neural decoder cannot become the de facto security boundary. Each
request records base and adapter hashes, service versions, corpus version,
policy version, token/tool budget, and every state transition.

\section{Experimental program}

\subsection{Phase 0: contracts and deterministic baselines}

Implement schema definitions, event logging, validators, ACL filtering,
capability checks, transactions, rollback, and the Aion state machine before
training. Construct deterministic BM25, workflow, static replanning, and routing
baselines. Freeze test generators and failure injections. Exit only when a
fresh process can replay a run from hashes and rejects malformed, unauthorized,
or post-hoc transitions.

\subsection{Phase 1: Hermes at 0.6B}

Pin and hash the selected base revision and license. Run supervised adapter
training at the repository's proposed bounded settings, but treat them as
hyperparameters rather than results. Compare base-only, prompting, and Hermes
on document-held-out grounding and out-of-domain calibration. Exit only with a
reproducible multi-seed advantage on the primary endpoint and no safety-gate
regression. A failed result is publishable evidence against specialization and
does not authorize a larger base.

\subsection{Phase 2: orthogonal capability tests}

Train Prometheus proposer/verifier and Perseus action/recovery adapters
independently. For Prometheus, freeze an equal-inference-token comparison. For
Perseus, reset environments per episode and score final state. Do not combine
them until both pass component and safety gates; otherwise integration obscures
root causes.

\subsection{Phase 3: knowledge and time}

Build \atlas{} as a retrieval experiment before any context extension. Freeze
corpus versions and ACL canaries. Only after a reliable evidence service exists
should Kronos compare Transformer-only, state-space hybrid, and static
replanning. Continual adaptation stays offline until deletion, retention, and
rollback tests pass.

\subsection{Phase 4: governed loop}

Run Aion's deterministic controller across bounded research tasks. Then add the
learned router in a paired, identical-budget evaluation. Pantheon audits both.
The learned variant is accepted only if its valid-experiment improvement is
accompanied by a predeclared non-inferiority bound on false promotion and zero
protected-data or authorization violations.

For all phases, use at least three seeds, bootstrap intervals over the true unit
of sampling, and report all preregistered outcomes. Family-level claims require
an independently executed reproduction after the development test is frozen.

\section{Threat model and reliability properties}

The system assumes the language model, retrieved text, tool output, and training
examples can all be adversarial. Threats include prompt injection, corpus
membership leakage, stale evidence, fabricated citation spans, schema-valid but
dangerous arguments, duplicate side effects, reward hacking, protocol drift,
held-out-answer access, checkpoint substitution, and self-promotion.

The principal controls are pre-retrieval ACL filtering, content-addressed
evidence, constrained decoding, deterministic semantic validation, least-
privilege tools, sandboxing, idempotency keys, two-phase material writes,
immutable logs, versioned protocols, separated proposer/verifier data,
sequestered tests, offline updates, atomic rollback, and human approval. These
controls do not prove security. They create inspectable failure points and
prevent the learned model from being the sole enforcement layer.

Reliability is defined over final states and receipts. An answer can be fluent
and wrong; an action can be valid JSON and unsafe; a run can succeed once and
fail under reset. Consequently, promotion requires replay from a fresh process,
environment reset, dependency and model identity checks, and retained failure
artifacts. Silent coercion of missing outputs to success is forbidden.

\section{Limitations and negative hypotheses}

The modular design may impose latency and coordination overhead. Shared weights
may create correlated errors between families; separate adapters do not ensure
independence. Small bases may lack the capacity needed for scientific synthesis
or robust tool recovery. Confidence heads can be miscalibrated out of domain.
Retrieval pipelines can amplify source bias. State-space encoders may not help
the sparse, irregular event streams available to Kronos. Deterministic Aion may
outperform a learned router precisely because its state space is well specified.

These are not implementation inconveniences; they are negative hypotheses the
program must be prepared to accept. The project should merge or delete a family
whose specialization does not beat the simplest valid baseline. It should not
rescue a weak claim by scaling parameters, changing metrics after evaluation,
or collapsing multiple roles into an uninspectable agent.

\section{Conclusion}

Olympus is most defensible as an evidence-bounded architecture program, not a
collection of prematurely named models. One shared decoder supports economical
specialization; explicit services preserve retrieval, action, time, and audit
boundaries; deterministic systems keep authority outside learned policies. Each
family has a primary hypothesis and an explicit rejection rule. The immediate
research direction is therefore contracts and baselines, followed by one
Hermes 0.6B adapter experiment. Prometheus and Perseus come next, \atlas{} and
Kronos only after their prerequisites, and Aion remains a governed controller
until learning demonstrates value. The proposal becomes a model paper only
after those experiments exist. Until then, its scientifically correct output is
the protocol itself.
